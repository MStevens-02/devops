# Team Code of Conduct
**Module:** SET08103 — Software Engineering Methods

## Purpose
This document sets out how we agree to work together as a team for the duration of this project. It exists to keep collaboration fair, communication clear, and to provide a clear reference point if disagreements arise.

---

## 1. Our Pledge
We are dedicated to maintaining a welcoming, safe, and productive environment where every team member is treated with respect and dignity. Our goal is to build top-tier products while actively encouraging continuous learning, career advancement, and mutual support.

---

## 2. Expected Behaviour & Communication
As a team, we agree to uphold the following standards based on the ACM and BCS Codes of Ethics:
- **Professionalism:** Communicate politely, respect differing viewpoints, and accept constructive feedback gracefully. Be respectful and constructive — critique ideas and code, not people.
- **Equality and Inclusivity:** All team members are equal, and every opinion is valuable. We work together openly, valuing and respecting everyone's input when making team decisions. No member should be excluded from discussions without valid, documented cause.
- **Role Rotation:** To ensure shared learning and practical experience across all areas of the project, leadership roles (Product Owner and Scrum Master) will be rotated periodically (every 2 weeks) among team members. All members contribute to development regardless of their current role.
- **Communication Standards:**
    - **Primary channel:** Discord/WhatsApp for day-to-day updates.
    - All major decisions (scope changes, task reassignment) must be posted in the group chat, not just discussed verbally, so there is an official record.
    - Respond to messages within **24 hours** where reasonably possible.
- **Commitment & Attendance:** Attend all scheduled Scrum meetings (Stand-ups during lab sessions, plus fixed team meetings every Tuesday and Friday). If absence is unavoidable, give advance notice to the team as early as possible.
- **Competence and Honesty:** Take on tasks within our capabilities, ask for help immediately when stuck (don't stay silent), and accurately report progress.
- **Fair Contribution:** Be an active contributor to the project. We commit to distributing the work fairly to ensure an equal contribution from everyone, which will be accurately reflected in the Individual Assessment spreadsheet.
- **Academic Integrity:** We commit to producing original work, avoiding plagiarism, and properly referencing any external sources or tools used during development.

---

## 3. Git, Workflow & Quality Standards
- **GitFlow Strategy:** Work happens on feature branches branching off `develop`. **Never commit directly to `master`.**
- **Pull Request Rules:** Every Pull Request must be reviewed and approved by at least one other team member before merging. **Self-merging is strictly prohibited.**
- **Commit Quality:** Write clear, descriptive commit messages. Keep commits reasonably small and frequent rather than dumping large blocks of code all at once.
- **Continuous Integration & Quality:** Do not push broken code to `develop`. Always check that the CI (GitHub Actions) is passing before merging. Code should be reasonably commented and follow agreed naming conventions.
- **Task Tracking:** Task assignments must be tracked on the Kanban/Project board (Zube.io / GitHub Projects) — pick up a task by assigning yourself, and don't leave things unclaimed.

---

## 4. Unacceptable Behaviour
In accordance with our University Student Charter and professional IT standards, the following behaviours are strictly prohibited:
- Any form of harassment, discrimination, or use of offensive language.
- Dismissing or silencing other team members' opinions or undermining their contributions.
- Repeatedly ignoring team communications or failing to deliver assigned tasks without valid reasons.
- Arriving unprepared or late for formal Code Review meetings.
- Misrepresenting one's contribution to the project or submitting falsified spreadsheet data.
- Bypassing the Pull Request review process (self-merging code).

---

## 5. Conflict Resolution and Disciplinary Process
If a team member is found to be violating this Code of Conduct, the following steps will be taken:
1. **Internal Review:** Disagreements should be raised directly and respectfully within the team first. The current Scrum Master and the team will have a private, constructive discussion with the individual to understand the underlying issues.
2. **Documented Warning:** If the behaviour persists, the team will formally document the lack of contribution or breach of conduct (e.g., via GitHub metrics, issue tracking, and chat logs).
3. **Escalation:** As a final resort, if the team cannot resolve an issue internally, the evidence will be presented to the module delivery team at the next available meeting. The individual will have the opportunity to present mitigating circumstances, but the module team retains the right to dismiss the individual, resulting in a failing grade.

---

## 6. Review of the Code
This Code of Conduct is a living document. It can be revisited, amended, and updated by mutual team agreement if it stops working effectively for us.
